How a project runs

No mystery, no change-order surprises.

Every project moves through the same four stages, and you get a written summary at the end of each one.

01 / Visit

Site walkthrough

Ninety minutes on site. We talk through how you want to use the space, then take grades, soil samples, and sun readings.

02 / Draw

Design study

Two directions, drawn to scale with real material samples in hand. You pick one and we refine it until it is right.

03 / Build

Construction

One crew, one foreman, a posted schedule. Daily cleanup, and a photo update at the end of every week.

04 / Tend

First five years

Scheduled pruning, irrigation tuning, and replacement of anything that fails to establish — at our cost.

Questions

The things everyone asks first.

What does a project typically cost?

Most full design-and-build projects we take on land between $38,000 and $145,000, depending on how much stone and grading is involved. Planting-only renovations usually run $12,000 to $30,000.

The design study is billed separately at $2,400 and is credited back in full if you build with us within a year.

How far out are you booking?

Design work starts within three to four weeks. Construction is typically eight to fourteen weeks out, and we schedule stonework in the drier months wherever the plan allows.

Do you work with my architect or builder?

Often, and happily. On new construction we prefer to be brought in during framing so we can set finished grades and sleeve for irrigation and lighting before the hardscape goes down.

What happens if a plant dies?

If it was installed by us and is on an Ironleaf care program, we replace it for the first three years at no charge — including the labour. Outside the care program the warranty is one year on plant material and five years on hardscape.

Are you able to work on small city lots?

Small lots are some of our favourite work — the constraints force better decisions. Our minimum project size is $12,000, which covers a typical courtyard or front garden rebuild.

Do you do maintenance for gardens you didn’t build?

Yes, with a one-time assessment visit first. We need to understand what is in the ground and what shape the irrigation is in before we can commit to a schedule.
