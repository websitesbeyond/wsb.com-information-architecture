Air conditioning repair

Flat-rate pricing, same-day appointments booked before 11am, and a diagnostic credited back when you approve the work.

When the house will not cool down

At 7,000 feet an air conditioner spends most of the summer coasting and then gets slammed for three weeks in July. That is exactly when the weak capacitor, the slow refrigerant leak, or the undersized return finally shows itself.

We start every call the same way: a full system check with measured readings — static pressure, temperature split, superheat and subcooling, amp draw on the compressor and blower. It takes about forty minutes and it is the difference between fixing the problem and replacing parts until the symptom goes away.

Our diagnostic is $89. It covers the full system check and a written report of what we measured. Approve a repair on the same visit and the $89 comes off the invoice. There is no after-hours, weekend or holiday surcharge on top of it.

Repairs we handle on the first visit

Our trucks carry the parts that fail in this climate, which is why 96% of repairs finish without a second trip:

  • Run and start capacitors, contactors and relays
  • Condenser fan motors and blower motors, including ECM modules
  • Refrigerant leak search, repair and proper weigh-in charge
  • Frozen evaporator coils and restricted airflow diagnosis
  • Clogged or cracked condensate drains and overflow switches
  • Thermostat replacement and miswired control circuits
  • Compressor hard-start kits and electrical fault repair
  • TXV and metering device replacement

What we will tell you honestly

If a fourteen-year-old condenser needs an $1,800 compressor, we will say so and price a replacement next to the repair so you can compare. If a four-year-old system needs a $220 part, nobody on our crew will try to sell you a new one. Our technicians are not paid on commission.

Symptoms

Six signs it is time to call

Most of these get worse and more expensive if the system keeps running.

01

Air is blowing, but warm

Usually a failed capacitor, a tripped condenser, or a system that has lost its refrigerant charge.

02

Ice on the lines or coil

Airflow restriction or low charge. Shut the system off and let it thaw before we arrive so we can read it properly.

03

Breaker keeps tripping

An electrical fault or a compressor drawing locked-rotor amps. Do not keep resetting it — that is how a repair becomes a replacement.

04

Short cycling

On for two minutes, off for three. Oversized equipment, a dirty coil or a failing control board.

05

Water around the air handler

A plugged condensate drain. Cheap to clear, expensive if it soaks drywall or a finished ceiling.

06

One room never cools

Nearly always duct design, not the equipment. We test airflow room by room before quoting anything.

AC repair questions

Good to know

Q Can you come out today?

If you book before 11am, yes, in nearly every case between May and September. After that we are usually scheduling next-morning windows. True emergencies — no cooling with an infant, an elderly resident or a medical need in the home — get moved to the front of the list.

Q Is the repair warrantied?

Two years on parts and labor for any repair we perform, which is double the industry norm. If the same part fails inside that window, the return visit and the replacement are on us.

Q Do you charge extra for nights or weekends?

No. The flat-rate book is the same at 2am on a Sunday as it is on a Tuesday morning. We would rather you call us early than run a failing system for three more days to avoid a surcharge.

Q My system is 15 years old — repair or replace?

Our rule of thumb: if the repair costs more than a third of a replacement and the system is past twelve years, replacement usually wins on total cost. We will quote both, show you the efficiency difference on your actual utility rate, and leave the decision with you.
